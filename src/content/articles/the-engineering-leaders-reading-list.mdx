Most engineering-leadership reading lists are just a pile of famous titles with no sense of *when* you'd actually need each one. The book that saves a brand-new manager is not the book that helps a leader-of-leaders redesign an organisation, and handing someone the wrong one at the wrong time is how good books get abandoned on page forty.

So I've grouped these by the problem they solve. Start with whichever section matches the problem in front of you right now.

## When you're making the leap

**_Engineering Manager for the Rest of Us_ - Sarah Drasner.** If you're a new or aspiring manager and most management books feel like they're written in a language you don't speak yet, start here. It's the rare EM book that trades jargon for practical, human advice - communication, people dynamics, feedback, the actual day-to-day.

**_The Manager's Path_ - Camille Fournier.** The closest thing our field has to a canonical map of the management career. Read the chapter for the level you're at now, then read the one above it to see what's coming.

## When you want to stay technical *and* lead

**_Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track_ - Will Larson.** A quiet act of career liberation. Larson makes the case that senior technical leadership is a real, respected path - not a consolation prize. If you're standing at the fork between the IC and management tracks (something I've [written about](/blog/engineering-management-in-the-netherlands-should-you-stay-technical-or-embrace-leadership/) from the Dutch market's rather blunt version of that choice), this is the book that dignifies the technical direction.

## When you're designing the organisation

**_An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management_ - Will Larson.** This is where management stops being about individuals and starts being about *systems*. It pairs naturally with the idea I keep returning to - that an organisation deserves the same careful, iterative design as a codebase.

**_Team Topologies_ - Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais.** The best vocabulary I know for talking about how teams should be shaped and how they should interact. Once you have this language, a whole class of organisational dysfunction becomes nameable, and therefore fixable.

## When you're accountable for delivery

**_Accelerate_ - Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble & Gene Kim.** The research backbone for measuring engineering performance without deluding yourself. This is where the DORA metrics come from, and it underpins a lot of what I argue in [measuring velocity without killing morale](/blog/measuring-velocity-without-killing-morale/).

## When culture is the real problem

**_The Fearless Organization_ - Amy Edmondson.** Edmondson is the researcher who put psychological safety on the map. It reframes safety not as niceness, but as [the hard prerequisite](/blog/engineering-leadership-beyond-delivery-why-psychological-safety-matters/) for learning, quality, and speed.

**_Turn the Ship Around!_ - L. David Marquet.** A submarine captain's account of moving from command-and-control to distributed intent and ownership. It's not a software book, which is exactly why it lands. If you struggle to stop being the bottleneck who approves everything, this one gets under your skin.

## How to actually use this list

Don't read all of these back to back. Reading management books is not the same as getting better at management. Instead: pick the section that matches the problem you have this month, read one book from it, and try exactly one thing from it with your team. Let it fail or stick. Then come back for the next one.
